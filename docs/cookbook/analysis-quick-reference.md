# Analysis quick reference

<!-- cookbook: reference -->

These public functions are imported from `fitness_landscape.analysis`; a “landscape” below means a `FitnessLandscape` with the graph and relevant fitness or annotation layer already selected.

| Area | Function and implementation | Input | What it does | Output | How to interpret it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accessibility | [`find_greedy_accessible_paths`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/adaptive_walk.py#L45) | A landscape with Hamming-style neighbour connections, one starting sequence, and one ending sequence. | Finds every simple route from the start to the end that increases fitness strictly at every step. | A dictionary containing the routes, their fitness values, the number of routes, and route-length summaries. | More routes indicate more observed strictly improving ways to reach the chosen endpoint. |
| Accessibility | [`analyze_path_accessibility`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/adaptive_walk.py#L135) | A landscape with Hamming-style neighbour connections and an active scalar fitness layer. | Identifies local low and high points and checks which low-to-high pairs are connected by at least one strictly improving route. | A dictionary containing the local minima and maxima, route counts for each pair, and the fraction of pairs that are accessible. | A larger accessible fraction means more observed low points can reach observed peaks without a fitness decrease. |
| Accessibility | [`calculate_basin_of_attraction_greedy`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/adaptive_walk.py#L221) | A landscape with Hamming-style neighbour connections and a sequence that is a local optimum. | Starts from every sequence, repeatedly moves to its highest-fitness neighbour, and records which starts reach the chosen optimum. | A dictionary containing basin members, basin size and fraction, and the optimum’s identity and fitness. | A larger basin fraction means the chosen optimum attracts more starting sequences under best-neighbour moves. |
| Accessibility | [`calculate_basin_of_attraction_stochastic`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/adaptive_walk.py#L324) | A landscape, a target optimum, and controls for the number and length of walks, selection strength, and the inclusion threshold. | Repeats random walks from every sequence, usually favouring fitter moves but sometimes accepting worse ones, and estimates the chance of reaching the target. | A dictionary containing reach probabilities for every start, thresholded basin members, basin size and fraction, and the simulation settings. | A larger basin fraction means more starts reach the target often enough under the chosen walk settings. |
| Accessibility | [`adaptive_walk_stochastic`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/adaptive_walk.py#L447) | A landscape, an optional starting sequence, a step limit, and either the best-neighbour or random-improving strategy. | Performs one fitness-increasing walk until no fitter neighbour is available or the step limit is reached. | A dictionary containing the visited nodes, sequence indices, fitness trace, step count, fitness gain, endpoint, and stopping flag. | A larger gain means that this particular walk improved more, while `reached_optimum` only means it stopped before the step limit and does not establish a global optimum. |
| Accessibility | [`neutral_network_analysis`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/adaptive_walk.py#L552) | A landscape and the largest fitness difference to treat as neutral. | Keeps connections between neighbours with sufficiently similar fitness and finds the connected groups they form. | A dictionary containing every neutral group, its size, fitness summary and diameter, plus the number and relative size of groups. | A larger largest-group fraction means more of the observed landscape can be traversed through fitness-neutral steps at the chosen threshold. |
| Ruggedness | [`calculate_ruggedness_dirichlet_energy`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/dirichlet_energy.py#L113) | A landscape with finite scalar fitness, plus optional edge-strength settings and edge-strength bins. | Adds the squared fitness change across every connected pair, optionally giving stronger connections more influence. | A dictionary containing summed energy, energy per node, weighting metadata, and optional energy totals and fractions by edge-strength bin. | Higher energy means connected sequences differ more in fitness and suggests greater ruggedness, but comparisons require the same fitness scale and network definition. |
| Ruggedness | [`local_dirichlet_energy_contribution`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/dirichlet_energy.py#L221) | A landscape with finite scalar fitness and an optional edge-strength field. | Assigns half of each connection’s fitness-difference energy to each of its two endpoints. | A dictionary mapping each node to its contribution, whose values sum to the global Dirichlet energy. | A larger value identifies a sequence whose immediate neighbourhood contributes more to the landscape’s abrupt fitness variation. |
| Ruggedness | [`graph_spectral_analysis`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph.py#L302) | A landscape, a choice of ordinary or degree-normalized network matrix, an optional number of modes, and edge-strength settings. | Breaks the network’s connectivity pattern into broad-to-local structural modes and measures how widely each mode is distributed across nodes. | A dictionary containing mode values, participation and localization scores, node contributions, a spectral gap when available, and a mode-value histogram. | A larger gap suggests stronger global connection and more localized modes flag structure concentrated in fewer sequences, but neither is itself a fitness-ruggedness score. |
| Epistasis | [`calculate_epistasis_walsh`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/epistasis.py#L24) | A complete set of all combinations of equal-length binary sequences, scalar fitness, and the highest interaction order to report. | Separates fitness into average, single-position, and multi-position effects for a complete binary landscape. | A dictionary containing effect coefficients grouped by order, relative contributions among the returned orders, design metadata, and summaries. | Larger absolute multi-position effects or larger higher-order contributions indicate stronger epistasis at those orders. |
| Epistasis | [`calculate_epistasis_regression`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/epistasis.py#L139) | An observed set of equal-length binary sequences, scalar fitness, an interaction order, and optional regression penalty settings. | Fits single-position and multi-position effects when some binary sequence combinations may be missing. | A dictionary containing fitted effects by order, in-sample fit quality, sample and rank information, identifiability status, and coefficient summaries. | Larger higher-order effects suggest stronger epistasis, but penalized effects are model-selected and an incomplete design may not identify them uniquely. |
| Epistasis | [`calculate_epistasis_ensemble`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/epistasis.py#L311) | Equal-length categorical or multi-allelic sequences, scalar fitness, and the highest interaction order to report. | Builds background-averaged effects from the genotype combinations that were actually observed without filling missing combinations. | A dictionary containing empirical effects grouped by order, observed-design coverage and balance metadata, decomposition details, and summaries. | Higher-order effects describe interactions in the observed sample and should be treated as a full population decomposition only for a complete balanced design. |
| Epistasis | [`calculate_epistasis_reference_free`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/epistasis.py#L357) | Equal-length categorical or multi-allelic sequences, scalar fitness, and the highest interaction order to report. | Calculates observed-sample effects without choosing one allele as the baseline and without filling missing genotype combinations. | A dictionary containing empirical effects grouped by order, observed-design coverage and balance metadata, decomposition details, and summaries. | Higher-order effects describe reference-free interactions in the observed sample and are not an orthogonal population decomposition when the design is incomplete or unbalanced. |
| Topology | [`graph_properties`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph.py#L20) | A landscape or an undirected NetworkX graph. | Summarizes how many neighbours nodes have, local clustering, typical route length, disconnected groups, and connection density. | A dictionary containing degree summaries, mean clustering, mean shortest-path length, component sizes, and density. | Use these values to describe sampling support and connectivity, noting that path length is calculated only on the largest component when the network is disconnected. |
| Ruggedness | [`calculate_ruggedness_local_optima`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph.py#L236) | A landscape with an active scalar fitness layer. | Finds every sequence with no fitter observed neighbour and summarizes their frequency and fitness. | A dictionary containing the local-optimum nodes and indices, their count and density, and their fitness summary. | A higher optimum density suggests more local peaks, but unobserved neighbours can make apparent peaks a sampling artefact. |
| Topology | [`resistance_distance_matrix`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph.py#L980) | A landscape or undirected graph, optional selected or sampled nodes, edge conductances, and optional categorical or annotation layers. | Measures how strongly each pair of nodes remains connected after accounting for all alternative routes and can summarize those distances between categories. | A dictionary containing sampled nodes, component membership, optional node-by-node distances, numerical diagnostics, and optional category-by-category distances. | Smaller finite distances indicate stronger and more redundant connectivity, while infinite distance means the nodes or category mass cross disconnected components. |
| Topology | [`category_diffusion_hierarchy`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph.py#L1328) | A landscape, one categorical fitness or annotation layer, and settings for the number of network coordinates and optional filtering. | Places nodes in coordinates that summarize network connectivity, averages those positions within categories, and clusters categories by their average positions. | A dictionary containing node coordinates, category centres and spreads, pairwise category distances, a clustering linkage and order, and filtering details. | Categories with closer centres occupy similar regions of the network, while larger within-category spread means a category is more dispersed. |
| Ruggedness | [`calculate_ruggedness_autocorrelation_analytical`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/random_walk.py#L177) | One connected non-trivial landscape with finite non-constant scalar fitness, a maximum step separation, and optional edge conductances. | Calculates exactly how similar fitness remains between positions separated by increasing numbers of steps in a stationary random walk. | A dictionary containing correlation at each integer separation, an optional lag-one equivalent decay length, and weighting metadata. | Correlation that stays strongly positive across more steps indicates smoother fitness variation, whereas rapid decay or oscillation indicates faster local change. |
| Ruggedness | [`time_continuous_autocorrelation`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/random_walk.py#L265) | One connected non-trivial landscape with finite non-constant scalar fitness, selected diffusion times, and optional edge conductances. | Calculates exactly how fitness similarity changes as a continuous-time random walk spreads through the network. | A dictionary containing correlation at each requested time and an elementary correlation time only for a single-mode signal. | More persistent positive correlation indicates smoother fitness over the network, and the returned time is diffusion time rather than a count of mutations. |
| Ruggedness | [`calculate_ruggedness_autocorrelation_stochastic`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/random_walk.py#L327) | One connected non-trivial landscape with finite non-constant scalar fitness, walk count and length, maximum separation, seed, and optional edge conductances. | Estimates fitness similarity over step separations by simulating stationary random walks. | A dictionary containing estimated correlation by integer separation, contributing pair counts, an optional lag-one equivalent decay length, and weighting metadata. | Slower positive decay suggests a smoother landscape, but uncertainty depends on the number and length of simulated walks. |
| Accessibility | [`category_boundary_crossing_times`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/random_walk.py#L410) | A landscape with a categorical fitness layer, simulation count and step limit, seed, and optional transition weights. | Simulates walks from each category and counts steps until they first reach every other category. | A dictionary containing category labels, mean and standard deviation of successful crossing times, hit counts, and simulation settings. | Shorter times suggest easier simulated movement between categories, but they must be read with hit counts because unsuccessful walks are omitted from the mean. |
| Ruggedness | [`compute_ruggedness_diffusion_scale`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/diffusion_scale.py#L732) | A landscape with a complete finite scalar fitness layer, a candidate scale range, and a grid, profile, bootstrap, or Laplace fitting method. | Fits how far a smoothing process must spread over the network to reproduce the observed distribution of fitness variation across broad and local patterns. | A dictionary containing the best-fitting diffusion scale, interval bounds, fit score at the best scale, and an uncertainty estimate. | A larger fitted scale indicates fitness variation concentrated in smoother network-wide patterns, while wide or boundary-touching intervals indicate weak scale identification. |
| Ruggedness | [`compute_ruggedness_variance_energy`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/diffusion_scale.py#L976) | A landscape with scalar fitness, either a chosen diffusion scale or settings used to fit one, and energy-weighting settings. | Builds the covariance expected under the diffusion model and calculates its expected local and global fitness-difference energy. | A dictionary containing the covariance matrix, expected energy by node and globally, the scale used, and node order. | The energies describe ruggedness expected under the chosen or fitted smoothing model and are not direct measurements of observed fitness differences. |
| Diffusion-scale fitting | [`fit_t_bayesian_laplace`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/diffusion_scale.py#L218) | An undirected graph, a scalar value for each node in graph order, scale bounds, and optional precomputed network modes. | Finds the most supported diffusion scale and approximates uncertainty from the local shape of the fit. | A five-value tuple containing the fitted scale, lower and upper approximate interval bounds, fit score, and approximate variance. | Larger scales represent smoother network patterns, and a broad approximate interval means the scale is poorly constrained. |
| Diffusion-scale fitting | [`fit_t_grid_posterior`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/diffusion_scale.py#L342) | An undirected graph, a scalar value for each node in graph order, scale bounds, grid size, prior choice, and optional precomputed network modes. | Evaluates a range of candidate diffusion scales and turns their relative support into a posterior distribution. | A five-value tuple containing the most supported scale, lower and upper credible bounds, fit score, and posterior variance. | Larger scales represent smoother network patterns, and a broad credible interval means many scales remain plausible under the chosen prior. |
| Diffusion-scale fitting | [`fit_t_profile_likelihood`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/diffusion_scale.py#L414) | An undirected graph, a scalar value for each node in graph order, scale bounds, grid size, confidence level, and optional precomputed network modes. | Evaluates candidate diffusion scales and keeps the range whose fit is sufficiently close to the best fit. | A five-value tuple containing the best-fitting scale, lower and upper confidence bounds, best fit score, and an interval-based variance estimate. | Larger scales represent smoother network patterns, and a broad confidence interval means the data do not distinguish scales sharply. |
| Diffusion-scale fitting | [`fit_t_bootstrap`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/diffusion_scale.py#L524) | An undirected graph, a scalar value for each node in graph order, scale-grid and prior settings, bootstrap count, seed, and optional precomputed network modes. | Fits a scale, simulates new signals from that fitted model, and refits each simulation to assess repeatability. | A five-value tuple containing the original fitted scale, bootstrap interval bounds, original fit score, and variance across bootstrap estimates. | Larger scales represent smoother network patterns, and a broad bootstrap interval means repeated signals from the fitted model give unstable scale estimates. |
| Network comparison | [`procrustes`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L89) | Two numeric coordinate arrays describing the same items with matching rows and dimensions. | Translates and rotates or reflects the second coordinate layout to match the first as closely as possible without rescaling it. | The aligned second array and one root-mean-square mismatch score. | A lower mismatch means the two layouts have more similar geometry after ignoring position and orientation. |
| Network comparison | [`edge_prf_on_observed`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L121) | A ground-truth graph, a reconstructed graph, and the nodes treated as observed. | Compares which connections among observed nodes are present in the truth and reconstruction. | Three numbers: edge precision, edge recall, and their combined F1 score. | Values closer to one indicate more accurate recovery of the observed-node connections. |
| Network comparison | [`sp_rmse`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L159) | Two graphs, a shared node list, and the edge field used as travel distance. | Compares the shortest travel distance between every node pair that has a finite route in both graphs. | One root-mean-square shortest-path difference. | A value closer to zero means the two graphs preserve similar travel distances among pairs connected in both graphs. |
| Network comparison | [`spectral_rmse`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L196) | Two graphs, the number of broad connectivity modes to compare, and optional edge conductances. | Compares the leading numerical summaries of broad-to-local connectivity in the two graphs. | One root-mean-square difference across the available mode values. | A value closer to zero means the graphs have more similar connectivity structure at the compared scales. |
| Network summary | [`edge_length_stats`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L227) | A graph and the edge attribute to summarize. | Collects the chosen value from every edge, using one when the attribute is missing, and describes its distribution. | A dictionary containing edge count, mean, median, and population standard deviation. | High and low values have meaning only after deciding whether the chosen edge attribute represents distance, conductance, similarity, or something else. |
| Network comparison | [`leaf_spanning_tree`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L249) | An undirected graph, nodes to connect as leaves, and an optional edge-distance field. | Extracts a compact connected subnetwork that joins the requested nodes, retaining the largest connected result if necessary. | A NetworkX graph containing the resulting leaf-spanning subnetwork. | The returned subnetwork shows the routes used to connect the requested nodes and is an intermediate structure rather than a goodness score. |
| Network comparison | [`leaf_splits`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L351) | A tree and the leaf labels to compare. | Removes each tree edge in turn and records the non-trivial division it creates among the chosen leaves. | A set of unique leaf groups representing the tree’s branch divisions. | Each returned group is one branching statement that can be compared between trees. |
| Network comparison | [`rf_distance`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L389) | Two trees and their shared leaf labels. | Counts the branch divisions that occur in one tree but not the other. | The raw differing-division count and a normalized score between zero and one. | Zero means the trees contain the same non-trivial divisions for those leaves, while larger values mean greater topological disagreement. |
| Network comparison | [`tree_rf_dissimilarity`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L417) | A truth landscape or graph, a reconstructed landscape or graph, optional shared leaves, and an edge-distance field. | Builds comparable trees spanning shared leaves and measures disagreement in their branching divisions. | A dictionary containing raw and normalized disagreement, leaf count, and the number of divisions in each tree. | A lower normalized value means the reconstruction better preserves the truth graph’s branching relationships among the selected leaves. |
| Network comparison | [`evaluate_reconstruction`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L511) | A ground-truth latent graph, its observed subgraph, and a reconstructed latent graph. | Runs a bundle of connection, shortest-path, connectivity-pattern, edge-value, and total-weight comparisons against the truth. | A dictionary containing edge precision, recall and F1, observed and reconstructed path errors, spectral error, edge-value summaries, and total weights. | Better reconstruction is indicated by higher edge scores and lower path and connectivity-pattern errors, with edge summaries used to diagnose why. |
| Network alignment | [`evaluate_isorank_alignment`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/graph_induction_alignment.py#L1659) | Two landscapes or graphs and settings controlling topology-versus-feature matching, iterations, feature sources, and connectivity modes. | Matches nodes between graphs using both their connections and available node features, then evaluates the matched connections and broad connectivity patterns. | A dictionary containing the node mapping, edge precision, recall and F1, connectivity-mode correlations, and graph and match sizes. | Higher edge scores and connectivity correlations indicate a stronger alignment under the chosen feature and topology settings. |
| Statistics | [`analyze_fitness_distribution`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/statistics.py#L161) | A landscape with scalar fitness, a significance threshold, and a missing-value policy. | Describes the location, spread, shape, percentiles, and histogram of fitness and runs a normality check when sample size and variation permit it. | A dictionary containing descriptive statistics, percentiles, shape measures, histogram bins, sample counts, and a normality-test record. | These values describe the active fitness sample, while a non-significant normality test does not prove that its population is normal. |
| Statistics | [`hypothesis_testing`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/statistics.py#L456) | At least two numeric groups supplied directly, extracted from named landscapes, or extracted from layers, plus test and multiple-testing settings. | Runs selected pairwise mean, rank, and distribution tests and corrects their p-values as one family. | A dictionary containing per-group summaries, pairwise test statistics, raw and adjusted p-values, significance flags, and correction metadata. | A small adjusted p-value indicates evidence of a group difference under that test’s assumptions, not the size or biological importance of the difference. |
| Statistics | [`permutation_test`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/statistics.py#L677) | At least two numeric groups, a two-group summary function, permutation count, alternative direction, seed, and multiple-testing settings. | Repeatedly shuffles group labels to estimate how unusual each observed pairwise difference would be if labels were exchangeable. | A mapping from each group pair to its observed value, raw and adjusted p-values, significance, simulation error, and reproducibility metadata. | A small adjusted p-value is evidence against exchangeable group labels for the chosen summary, provided the observations are exchangeable under the null. |
| Robustness | [`subsample_analysis`](../../src/fitness_landscape/analysis/statistics.py#L911) | A landscape, an analysis function, numbers and proportions of nodes and edges to retain, an optional layer and seed, and parallel-execution settings. | Repeats an analysis on connected subsamples of the observed network without reconstructing edges after sampling. | A dictionary containing every subsample result and, for scalar or consistent numeric outputs, means, sample standard deviations, and percentile intervals. | Similar results and narrow intervals across subsamples indicate that the chosen analysis is less sensitive to the sampled nodes and edges. |
